How HMAC works

HMAC (Hash-based Message Authentication Code) combines a secret key with a message to produce a short digest that proves both the message's integrity (it wasn't altered) and its authenticity (it was produced by someone who holds the secret key). The construction is HMAC(K, m) = H((K ⊕ opad) ∥ H((K ⊕ ipad) ∥ m)), where H is the underlying hash (SHA-256, SHA-384, or SHA-512) and the key is XOR'd with inner/outer padding constants. This double-hashing prevents length-extension attacks that would affect plain H(K ∥ m). Verification uses a constant-time comparison of the computed HMAC against the expected one to prevent timing oracle attacks.
