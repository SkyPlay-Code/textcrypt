How LSB stego detection works

LSB (Least Significant Bit) steganography embeds secret data into the lowest bit of each pixel channel. The LSB plane is extracted by isolating only the least significant bit of each R, G, or B channel and amplifying it (×255) for visibility — a clean image shows gradual patterns matching the image content, while a stego image shows a noisy/random-looking pattern. Chi-square analysis tests whether the distribution of pixel values (before LSB modification) is consistent with random embedding: stego tools tend to produce a very even 50/50 split of 0 and 1 LSBs across pixel pairs (Pairs of Values, or PoV attack). A ratio very close to 50% and high LSB entropy are indicators of possible hidden data. However, this is heuristic — compressed JPEGs and natural images with smooth gradients can produce false positives, and steganography tools that use fewer channels or partial embedding may evade this analysis.
