How the Linguistic / Markov Disguiser works

Unlike zero-width Unicode or raw LSB bit flipping which leave telltale entropy spikes, Linguistic Steganography converts binary data into grammatical word choices across a deterministic Context-Free Grammar (CFG) / N-gram model.

1. The payload is framed with a length header and a 16-bit CRC checksum (and optionally encrypted with AES-GCM).
2. The resulting bitstream steps through syntactic sentence templates (e.g. [Transition] [Subject Adj] [Subject] [Adverb] [Verb] [Object Adj] [Object] [Preposition] [Target Adj] [Target]).
3. Each syntactic slot draws from a power-of-two dictionary ($2^k$ vocabulary choices = $k$ bits). Each well-formed sentence naturally carries 35+ bits of information without producing high-entropy gibberish, spam anomalies, or invisible characters.
