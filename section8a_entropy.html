How Shannon entropy works

Shannon entropy H = -Σ p(x) × log₂(p(x)) measures the average unpredictability of each byte, in bits. Maximum entropy (8.0 bits/byte) means every byte value is equally likely — indistinguishable from random, as produced by encryption or compression. Plaintext (English text) scores ~3.5–5.5 bits/byte because letters like 'e','t','a' appear far more often than others — the distribution is uneven. Compressed data (zip, gzip, jpg) scores 7.5–8.0 because compression removes redundancy, making the output appear more random. Encrypted data also scores 7.5–8.0, but unlike compressed data it has no internal structure or file headers — though distinguishing the two from byte frequency alone is not always possible.
